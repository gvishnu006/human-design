How you arrive

The nine profiles

Your profile is your type crossed with the line of your personality — the angle from which you look at life. Select any profile to read its theme.

Three of the nine are transitional profiles — their emphasis is shaped largely by life experience, which is why you can see the same profile behave very differently in two people.

The inner lens

The four lines

Your line describes the private emotional job you came here to do. It rarely changes, and it is the reason two people with identical types feel nothing alike.

①

Perspective

“I want to see things from the top.”

You are a natural observer who collects vantage points. Your gift is patience and perspective — and your risk is disengaging from the action while you gather it.

②

The Remote

“My knowing is private until it is shared.”

You work on instinct and trust slowly. Your gift is knowing things before you can justify them — and your risk is staying silent long after you already had the answer.

③

The Mutation

“Next, not now.”

You are wired for change and distrust the gradual. Your gift is genuine reinvention — and your risk is starting something you abandon before it lands.

④

The Intimate

“With the right few, I show up fully.”

You are built for real closeness, not broad contact. Your gift is depth and trust — and your risk is holding your standards so high that no one gets close enough.

Your not-self theme

Every type has an emotional state it falls into when it ignores its own authority. Recognising it is the fastest way to catch yourself out of alignment — you will usually know within seconds.

  • Generator & Manifestor → Frustration — agitation from pushing before the body agrees.
  • Projector & Rebel → Success — bitter irony from recognition you did not ask for.
  • Reflector → Disappointment — the low feeling of a decision that was made too early.
  • Any type, in short-term stress → Anger — the reactive layer underneath the real theme.
Where feeling lives

The nine energy centres

Centres are where you experience the world. A defined centre amplifies and holds; an open one borrows and magnifies whatever moves through it. Select a centre to read its role.

Head

Thinking centre

Your centre of questions. When defined, you have an endless supply of curiosity and an opinion about everything — exhausting for others, indispensable for you. When open, you pick up other people's thinking, and the trick is asking your own question before answering theirs.

If defined
Constant analysis; you need to think before you speak.
If open
Absorbs ambient ideas and treats them as yours.
Key move
Move your mouth to listen to yourself think out loud.

Throat

Expression centre

Your centre of speech and self-expression. It is the throat, not the head, that makes things real — a realisation people usually arrive at too late. Defined, you have a calling you are meant to be honest about. Open, you are prone to manifesting in the moment and regretting the wording.

If defined
Clear sense of purpose; you can be candid without fear.
If open
Chokes on unsent truth; speaks the wrong thing on impulse.
Key move
Pause, then say the accurate thing.

Ajna

Clarity centre

Your visual and mental clarity. With Ajna defined you can see the shape of a problem instantly and explain it better than most specialists. With it open you think in bursts, and clarity arrives only in the shower, the walk, or mid-sentence.

If defined
Rapid pattern recognition; explains things well.
If open
No clarity until the moment after you needed it.
Key move
Write it out; the clarity survives being said once.

Heart

Worth centre

Your centre of self-worth and value. A defined Heart wants to be valued for what it creates, not for what it produces on paper. An open Heart takes its sense of worth from other people, which makes it the most easily manipulated centre in the system.

If defined
Self-worth fluctuates between feeling valuable and empty.
If open
Mirrors others' approval; you can be talked into your value.
Key move
Decide from conviction, not from being tapped on the shoulder.

Solar Plexus

Emotion centre

Your centre of feeling and instinct. Defined, you have a reliable emotional wave you can ride: wave builds, peaks, subsides. Open, you amplify the emotional weather of whoever is nearest, and that is a talent in the right room and a liability in the wrong one.

If defined
Wait out emotional lows; truth arrives with the wave.
If open
Adopts other people's moods, sometimes without noticing.
Key move
Ask “is this mine?” before acting on the feeling.

Sacral

Drive centre

Your centre of life force and work. It responds to enthusiasm, not to mental conviction. A defined Sacral answers questions with a spoken yes or no and a distinctive huh-huh — historically called the “yes or no gene.” Open, you take on other people's drive and enthusiasm without noticing.

If defined
Steady reserves; must respond to what you enjoy.
If open
Unreliable reserves; prone to dissatisfaction loops.
Key move
Talk while you wait for the answer to arrive in the body.

Root

Pressure centre

Your centre of drive, pressure and survival. A defined Root is a rocket — a fixed amount of pressure and a predictable rhythm. An open Root has no internal motor, so you absorb urgency from the people and environments around you.

If defined
Consistent pressure; a reliable work rhythm.
If open
Takes on other people's deadlines and stress.
Key move
Never hurry on its behalf; let the pace come to you.

Identity

Self centre

Your centre of direction, awareness and who you are. Often called the third eye in colloquial use, though in Human Design the Ajna is the visual centre. A defined Identity means your sense of self is consistent and available; an open one means you take on the identity of whoever you are around.

If defined
Clear, stable self-direction you can name.
If open
Shifts shape with each new environment or person.
Key move
Keep a private practice that has nothing to do with others.

Emotional / Body

Tempo centre

Your centre of tempo and emotional truth. It is also the heart of the system: where it is defined, your inner authority lives. Nothing is decided here in a single moment — truth has a settling time, and a decision taken too early is the source of most Projector and Rebel regret.

If defined
Inner authority; one clear answer after a wave.
If open
It is not your authority — your type's centre is.
Key move
Sleep on it. Then sleep on it again if it is a real decision.
The part that actually matters

Your inner authority

Everything above is context. This is the part you use. Your authority is the specific centre in your chart that is entitled to answer the question — and consulting any other one is how good people talk themselves out of the right decision.

Sacral Authority

Body-level yes or no

Found in most Generators and Manifestors. Your authority lives below the diaphragm.

  • Get clear on what you actually want to know.
  • Ask out loud, with a yes/no question.
  • Let the body answer before your head does.
  • Responded to — the huh-huh is a yes. Then wait and ask again.

Body language is the only reliable reading here. Do not translate a yes into a why.

Emotional Authority

Nothing decided in a moment

Common for Projectors and Rebels. Your authority is the emotional body itself.

  • Feel your way to a genuine truth — not a comfortable one.
  • Recognise when the wave has crested and stopped moving.
  • Only then claim the answer, without second-guessing it.
  • Move immediately. Energy spent on doubts at this stage is wasted.

Satisfaction and peace are the signals. Never make a big decision while emotionally flooded.

Head Authority

Questions are the input

For the rarer charts where the Head is defined and no other inner authority is active.

  • There is no answer until you are asking the right question.
  • Outward curiosity feeds you; fixed conclusions do not.
  • Keep a running list of what is genuinely unresolved.
  • One genuine question can restructure a decade.

You are licensed to be uncertain. The search is the work, and certainty is not the point.

A usable decision loop

  1. Check the centre. Which authority is defined in your chart? Everything below follows from that one answer.
  2. Name the real question. Most bad decisions come from answering an easier question.
  3. Consult your authority, not your logic. Logic is a tool you apply afterwards, to the answer you already have.
  4. Watch the signature. Frustration, success or disappointment is the signal that you skipped the loop. Use it as a prompt, not a punishment.
  5. Then commit without drama. Detachment is not doubt. A Human Design decision is not revisited weekly.
